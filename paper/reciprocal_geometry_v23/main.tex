\textbf{Abstract.}
We give two geometric realizations of fifth-order iterations for positive roots. A prepared fixed circle inverts a quadratic Pad\'e model. Its selected real branch strictly reduces logarithmic error and alternates across the root; a short convexity argument proves convergence throughout the branch for every real $p>2$. For integer degrees, a regular update uses $2p+2$ joins, including the power stage. A second construction uses a decentered arc and converges monotonically from every positive start, also with exact order five. We compare their formulas with classical rational and quadratic approximation, derive explicit mobile-work bounds, and give a global circle--AD controller. Lean certificates and independent incidence computations support the analysis. The contribution is the specified constructions, their domains and their costs; fifth-order approximation itself is classical.

\section{Two solvers, one construction problem}
A formula specifies arithmetic; a geometric calculator must realize it. A product can be a pair of projections, while a square root can be a circle crossing. We ask how cheaply a positive-root correction can be realized with prepared lines and circles, and on which states the resulting iteration converges.

The paper has three main results, stated here with their precise roles:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fixed-circle solver.} A quadratic inverse model has exact order five and strictly contracts logarithmic error on its entire real branch (Theorems~\ref{thm:circlelocal} and~\ref{thm:branch}). The proof works uniformly for real $p>2$.
\item \textbf{Decentered radical solver.} A moving arc realizes a different fifth-order correction, globally monotone on $(0,\infty)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:arc}). Its support uses the same mobile operations as the centered order-three AD support.
\item \textbf{Explicit geometric cost.} For integer $p\ge3$, the prepared circle needs $2p+2$ joins in its finite regular chart (Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}). AD entry and chart fallback give a global algorithm with separately counted control work (Proposition~\ref{thm:entry}).
\end{enumerate}
The two dynamics are deliberately distinguished: circle steps cross the root with decreasing error; arc steps stay on the initial side. Rational Pad\'e supplies a classical fifth-order comparator. Section~\ref{sec:prior} identifies the inherited approximation theory before the constructions are developed. The classical rectangle supports, historical reference and exploratory analog implementation are retained in the appendices.

\section{Position relative to existing methods}\label{sec:prior}
\subsection{Classical formulas and geometric interpretation}
High local order is not a novelty claim. Traub's Pad\'e organization of iteration formulas already unifies several classical schemes and their error terms \cite{traub1961,traub1964}. Gander treats both rational Halley and the radical Euler correction obtained by solving a quadratic Taylor model \cite[pp.~131--133]{gander1985}. Scavo--Thoo develop the geometry of Halley's method \cite{scavo1995}; Melman connects geometric interpretation with global convergence \cite{melman1997}. Geometric constructions for nonlinear iteration also appear in Amat--Busquier--Guti\'errez \cite{amat2003}. Thus neither geometric iteration, global convergence via geometry, nor a radical correction is claimed here as a new general principle.

Our normalization is $a=X^{-1/p}$, $t=Xs^p$ and $s^+=s\Phi(t)$. With $x=s/a$, the normalized iteration is $x^+=x\Phi(x^p)$. Centered AD is exactly the classical $h_{11}$, projective AD is $h_{12}$, and the rational fifth-order baseline is $h_{22}$ in the Pad\'e family of Laszkiewicz--Zi\k{e}tak \cite{laszkiewicz2009,zietakSlides2009}; the latter two formulas are printed on slide~11 of the authors' presentation. Karp--Lin prove convergence results for this rational family \cite{karp2011}. We use these maps as identified baselines, with new coordinates and explicit incidence inventories where applicable.

The construction question also differs from existence under the Poncelet--Steiner theorem: a fixed circle and its center allow straightedge constructions \cite{steiner1833}, but a particular iterative protocol still needs its coordinates, admissible branch and work inventory. Our inventory follows the constructive concerns of geom\'etrographie and nomography \cite{lemoine1902,docagne1925}; it is an upper bound under stated primitives, not a minimum-trace theorem.

\subsection{Inverse Pad\'e and quadratic approximation}\label{sec:quadraticprior}
The order-five rational comparator approximates $t^{-1/p}$ directly. The fixed circle instead approximates $v^{-p}$ by $N(v)/D(v)$ and then solves $N(v)-tD(v)=0$. These operations are not interchangeable. With the coefficients defined in~\eqref{eq:model}, writing the circle equation as
\[
 (\gamma_p-t\alpha_p)v^2+2\beta_p(t-1)v+(\alpha_p-t\gamma_p)=0
\]
places it within the general algebraic form $A(t)v^2+B(t)v+C(t)=0$ associated with quadratic approximation. Shafer already develops quadratic approximation as an alternative to rational Pad\'e when square roots are available \cite{shafer1974}. The use of such an algebraic approximant therefore has a direct classical precedent.

The decentered correction belongs to the same broad algebraic class. Put
\[
 S(t)=(p-2)(t^2+1)+(10p+4)t.
\]
Eliminating the positive radical from~\eqref{eq:arc} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcimplicit}
 (p-1)^2 S(t)(v-1)^2-12p(tv-1)^2=0.
\end{equation}
Squaring loses the sign information: the explicit positive radical in~\eqref{eq:arc}, rather than this polynomial alone, defines the iteration. In particular the equation has a double root at $t=1$. We do not identify it with a particular normalized Shafer approximant solely from its quadratic form.

There is also a direct, checkable distinction between the three fifth-order maps in the same logarithmic error $e=\log(s/a)$:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}\toprule
Correction & Coefficient of $e^5$ in $e^+$\\\midrule
Fixed-circle inverse & $-(p^2-1)(p^2-4)/720$\\
Decentered arc & $(p-2)(p^2-1)(3p+1)/1440$\\
Rational diagonal Pad\'e & $(p^2-1)(4p^2-1)/720$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The coefficients are pairwise distinct for $p>2$. This proves nonidentity with the specified rational baseline in the stated normalization; it is not a classification of all algebraic iterations or coordinate conjugacies.

\textbf{Comparison with tangent-circle methods.} Kanwar--Singh--Bakshi include osculating-circle and ellipse constructions among their quadratic and cubic families \cite{kanwar2008}. Pakdemirli's recent circular-arc algorithm uses the tangent circle with the function's local curvature \cite{pakdemirli2025}. Our fixed circle is prepared once for the degree; its intersections encode a residual-dependent quadratic solve. Our decentered arc also has a fixed center, with radius prescribed by the residual. Neither is obtained by fitting the curvature of the graph at the current state. This construction-level distinction does not claim priority over every possible circle encoding.

\textbf{Scope of the contribution.} We claim the explicit incidence realizations and prove the stated branch/global dynamics and protocol bounds. Their formulas are positioned inside classical approximation theory. The source ledger (\texttt{LITERATURE\_REVIEW.md}) records the full texts, formulas and abstracts consulted. A bounded literature comparison cannot establish historical priority, and no universal ``first geometric solver'', optimal trace count or electrical speedup is asserted.

\section{Coordinates as reusable computing elements}\label{sec:rails}
Let $X>0$, let $p\ge3$ be an integer, and write
\[
 a=X^{-1/p},\qquad t=Xs^p,\qquad s^+=s\Phi(t),\qquad s>0.
\]
The state approaches the reciprocal root $a$; inversion supplies the desired $X^{1/p}$. We use $e=\log(s/a)$ for local error. Points are capital letters, scalar iteration maps are $\map{label}$, and polynomial coefficients use Greek letters. Thus a point and a correction function never share the same symbol.

In a rectangle with vertices $C=(0,0)$, $B=(W,0)$, $O=(0,H)$ and $A=(W,H)$, define two extended rails and a family of top centers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rails}
 R(q)=(W,H(1-q)),\quad L(q)=(0,H(1-q)),\quad
 \hub{f}=\left(\frac{Wf}{f-1},H\right).
\end{equation}
A line through the prepared center performs multiplication in one direction and division in the other:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:railops}
 \hub{f}R(q)\cap OC=L(fq),\qquad
 \hub{f}L(b)\cap AB=R(b/f).
\end{equation}
For example, substituting the first two points into a line equation and setting $x=0$ gives ordinate $H(1-fq)$. This is the whole arithmetic content of the rail device.

\subsection{The power stage}
Start at $P=R(s)$. The join $CP$ meets the top line at $V=\hub{1/s}$. A leftward projection through $\hub{1/2}$ followed by a return through $V$ changes a rail value $q$ to $sq/2$. After $p-1$ pairs, the endpoint is $Q=R(s^p/2^{p-1})$. Including the initial join, the stage uses $2p-1$ lines. Its normalization factor is part of the design, not discarded arithmetic.

More generally, choosing the leftward factors $f_1,\ldots,f_{p-1}$ gives an endpoint with value $s^p\prod_i f_i$. This allows the power stage to feed a device with its preferred residual scale. We use exactly this freedom for the fixed-circle calculator.


\textbf{Power protocols, one state encoding.} The later rectangle drawings use a different power stage: similar triangles and parallels produce $E=R(s^p)$ directly. They do not change the meaning of $R(s)$. The rail telescope instead produces a scaled power; its scale is absorbed into the prepared readout. Their costs must be kept separate.

\subsection{A precise notion of work}
We count a join as $J$, a parallel-construction macro as $P_{\parallel}$, and a mobile compass arc as $C_{\mathrm{arc}}$. Intersections of already drawn objects, fixed preparation and final inversion are excluded. When expanded into ruler-and-compass traces, our parallel macro costs $2J+3C_{\mathrm{arc}}$. These conventions are specified before any cost comparison.

The name Pandrosion refers to the addressee of the opening of Book III of Pappus' \emph{Collection} \cite{pappusLittle2023}. Our rectangle follows a modern reconstruction \cite{launayPandrosion}; the accelerated supports are modern constructions, not attributed to the ancient text.

\section{A prepared circle as a quadratic solver}\label{sec:circle}
Suppose the desired correction factor is $v\approx1$. Instead of evaluating a rational approximation to $t^{-1/p}$, approximate the inverse relation $t=v^{-p}$ by a quadratic rational model and solve it exactly. Put
\begin{align}
 \alpha_p&=(p+1)(p+2),&\beta_p&=p^2-4,&\gamma_p&=(p-1)(p-2),\nonumber\\
 N(v)&=\gamma_pv^2-2\beta_pv+\alpha_p,&
 D(v)&=\alpha_pv^2-2\beta_pv+\gamma_p.\label{eq:model}
\end{align}
The model is $\mathcal R(v)=N(v)/D(v)$, the $[2/2]$ Pad\'e approximant of $v^{-p}$ at one. Its inverse branch $v(1)=1$ defines $\map{circle}(s)=sv(Xs^p)$. A circle can supply the quadratic root in a single crossing.

\subsection{The eight-line cubic example}
Use $W=2$, $H=4$, $p=3$ and $X=2$. Prepare a circle through $B$ with center $O_\Gamma$, a projector $Z$, and constants
\[
 O_\Gamma=(-152/113,126/113),\quad Z=(478/113,396/113),
 \qquad \rho=\tfrac12,\quad k=\tfrac5{113}.
\]
The first five joins form the rail power stage with left factors $\rho$ and $\sigma=20/113$, retaining $L_1=L(\rho s)$ and ending at $T=R(kXs^3)$. The last three joins are the main device:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw $ZT$ and select its prescribed intersection $G$ with the fixed circle.
\item Draw $BG$ to the top line, obtaining $U=\hub{\rho/v}$.
\item Draw $UL_1$ back to the right rail. Its value is $(\rho s)/(\rho/v)=sv$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the inverse correction has order five}
At $v=1$, $N=D=12$. The polynomial certificate for local order is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
 pND+v(N'D-ND')=p\alpha_p\gamma_p(v-1)^4.
\end{equation}
Moreover $\gamma_pN=(\gamma_pv-\beta_p)^2+3(p^2-4)$ and $\alpha_pD=(\alpha_pv-\beta_p)^2+3(p^2-4)$, so the logarithms used below are well defined for $p>2$.

\begin{theorem}[Local inverse-circle order]\label{thm:circlelocal}
For real $p>2$, the inverse branch through $v=1$ exists near $t=1$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circleorder}
 e^+=-\frac{(p^2-1)(p^2-4)}{720}e^5+O(e^7).
\end{equation}
It therefore alternates sides locally and has exact order five.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $v=1+u$, $E(u)=\log\mathcal R(1+u)/p$ and $K(u)=E(u)+\log(1+u)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:cert} gives
\[
 K'(u)=\frac{\alpha_p\gamma_pu^4}{(1+u)N(1+u)D(1+u)}.
\]
Thus $K(u)/u^5\to\alpha_p\gamma_p/720$, while $E(0)=0$ and $E'(0)=-1$. The inverse function theorem identifies $E(u)$ with the input logarithmic error. Dividing the limits gives the coefficient in~\eqref{eq:circleorder}. Analyticity and $\mathcal R(1/v)=1/\mathcal R(v)$ make the logarithmic update odd, giving the stated remainder. The coefficient is nonzero for $p>2$.
\end{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:cert} specializes a classical Pad\'e differential-defect identity \cite[proof of Theorem~2.4]{karp2011}; integrating it proves contact with $v^{-p}$.

\subsection{Which circle intersection?}
The discriminant and the descending branch $\mathcal R'(v)<0$ are

 \Delta_p(t)&=(42p^2-24)t-3(p^2-4)(1+t^2),\\
 \tau_\pm&=\frac{7p^2-4\pm4p\sqrt{3(p^2-1)}}{p^2-4},\qquad \tau_-\tau_+=1,\\
 v_\pm&=\frac{p^2+2\pm\sqrt{12(p^2-1)}}{p^2-4}.
\end{align*}
The transverse real interval is $\tau_-<t<\tau_+$; select its inverse with $v_-<v<v_+$.

\subsection{The general-degree construction}\label{sec:generalcircle}
For each integer $p\ge3$, prepare $O_\Gamma=(h,j)$, $Z=(z_x,z_y)$, $k>0$ and $\rho>0$, using the rational formulas in Appendix~\ref{app:prep}. The circle passes through $B=(2,0)$ and is independent of $X$ and $s$. Put $\sigma=kX2^{p-3}/\rho$. The rail sequence uses
\[
 f_1=\rho,\quad f_i=\tfrac12\ (2\le i\le p-2),\quad f_{p-1}=\sigma.
\]
It saves $L_1=L(\rho s)$ and ends at $T=R(kt)$, because $\prod_i f_i=kX$. The same three final joins then give $R(sv)$.

\begin{theorem}[Counted finite-circle protocol]\label{thm:construction}
For the preparation in Appendix~\ref{app:prep}, assume $X,s>0$, $\tau_-<Xs^p<\tau_+$, and
\[
 s\ne1,\quad \sigma\ne1,\quad v\ne\rho,\quad
 b(v)=(2-h)2v-4j(\rho-v)\ne0.
\]
The selected branch is realized by $2p+2$ mobile joins using the prepared circle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The power stage costs $1+2(p-1)=2p-1$ joins. To certify the remaining incidence, write
\[
 d=(2v,4(\rho-v)),\quad n=d\cdot d,\quad b=(B-O_\Gamma)\cdot d,
 \quad G=B-\frac{2b}{n}d.
\]
The pencil line through $B$ has direction $d$. Substituting $B+\xi d$ into the circle equation gives $\xi(\xi n+2b)=0$: besides $B$, the other intersection is exactly the displayed $G$. Thus $b\ne0$ keeps the two points distinct. The same line meets the top at $\hub{\rho/v}$ when $v\ne\rho$.

It remains to show that the projector $Z$ and the residual point $T$ select this $G$. Three points are collinear exactly when the determinant of their displacement vectors vanishes. Multiplying that determinant by $n$ gives
\begin{align*}
 I={}&(2-z_x)(-2bd_y-z_yn)\\
 &-[4(1-kt)-z_y][(2-z_x)n-2bd_x].
\end{align*}
The prepared parameters satisfy $I=\lambda_p[N(v)-tD(v)]$, with $\lambda_p\ne0$. This identity converts the geometric incidence into exactly the inverse model, rather than an approximation to it. Since the selected root has $N(v)-tD(v)=0$, it places $G$ on $ZT$. Projecting $G$ through $B$, then $L_1$ through the resulting center, gives $R(sv)$ by~\eqref{eq:railops}. These are three joins. The branch arc is parameterized by $v_-<v<v_+$ and can be marked during preparation using its endpoints and $G(1)$. The listed hypotheses exclude an infinite center, a coincident $B,G$, and tangency; the other rail intersections are transverse.
\end{proof}
The exclusions concern this finite chart. In particular, $s=1$ sends the initial center $V$ to infinity even though the scalar correction can remain regular. A positive rescaling $\widetilde X=c^pX$, $\widetilde s=s/c$ preserves $t$ and conjugates every correction. A dyadic choice can keep the limiting state away from one, controlling the distant initial center near convergence. The circle-specific exclusions still require their own branch and chart checks.

The result is a concrete upper bound on mobile work, not merely an existence theorem for straightedge construction. Its advantage over the rational fifth-order baseline will be quantified after the other devices have been introduced.

\subsection{Uniform contraction on the whole real branch}\label{sec:branch}
Here ``contraction'' means strict error reduction relative to the root. No pairwise Lipschitz constant is required. A Cayley coordinate makes the whole-branch argument independent of integer-degree estimates.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-branch contraction and alternation]\label{thm:branch}
Let $p>2$ be real and $\tau_-<t<\tau_+$, $t\ne1$. On the descending inverse branch $v=v(t)$, put $e=\log t/p$ and $e^+=e+\log v$. Then
\[
 ee^+<0,\qquad |e^+|<|e|.
\]
Consequently the scalar circle iteration stays in this residual band and converges to $X^{-1/p}$ from every start in it. Its exact local order is five.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By reciprocity it suffices to consider $v>1$, so $t<1$. Set
\[
 x=\frac{v-1}{v+1},\qquad x_* =\sqrt{\frac{3}{p^2-1}},\qquad
 y=\frac{3px}{3+(p^2-1)x^2}.
\]
The branch condition becomes $0<x<x_*<1$. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayleybranch}
 \frac{N(v)}{D(v)}=\frac{1-y}{1+y},\qquad
 \log v=2\artanh x,\qquad -\log t=2\artanh y.
\end{equation}
Positivity of $N,D$ gives $0<y<1$. The branch inequality also gives $y>px/2$; hence $0<x<px/2<1$ because $p>2$.

The function $f(z)=\artanh z$ is strictly convex on $(0,1)$ and $f(0)=0$; indeed $f''(z)=2z/(1-z^2)^2>0$. Thus $f(cz)>cf(z)$ whenever $c>1$ and $0<cz<1$. Applying this with $c=p/2$ yields the entire upper-error estimate in one line:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convexity}
 \artanh y>\artanh(px/2)>\frac p2\artanh x,
 \qquad p\log v< -2\log t.
\end{equation}
This proves $e^+<-e$. To prove that the step crosses the root, use the already established defect certificate~\eqref{eq:cert}:
\[
 \frac{d}{dv}\log\!\left(v^p\mathcal R(v)\right)
 =\frac{p\alpha_p\gamma_p(v-1)^4}{vN(v)D(v)}>0\quad(v>1).
\]
The logarithm is zero at $v=1$, so $p\log v+\log t>0$ and $0<e^+<-e$. Replacing $(t,v)$ by $(1/t,1/v)$ proves the other half.

For convergence put $z=\log t$ and $F(z)=\log[tv(t)^p]$. The inverse branch is continuous, $F(0)=0$, and $|F(z)|<|z|$ for $z\ne0$. Reciprocity makes the band symmetric in $z$. An orbit starting at $z_0$ stays in the compact interval $[-|z_0|,|z_0|]$ inside that band. If its decreasing absolute errors had positive limit $\ell$, a convergent subsequence $z_{n_k}\to z_*$ would satisfy $|z_*|=|F(z_*)|=\ell$, contradicting the strict inequality. Thus $z_n\to0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:circlelocal} supplies the local order.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint margin and proof coverage}\label{sec:endpoint}
The same argument settles the endpoint inequality without degree splitting. At $x=x_*$, one has $y_*=px_*/2<1$, $v_+=(1+x_*)/(1-x_*)$ and $\tau_+=(1+y_*)/(1-y_*)$. Strict convexity gives
\[
 2\log\tau_+-p\log v_+
 =4\artanh(px_*/2)-2p\artanh x_*>0.
\]
Thus $v_+^p<\tau_+^2$ for every real $p>2$. No rational brackets for $\sqrt{12(p^2-1)}$, finite list of degrees or exponential numerical bound is needed. Scalar endpoint contraction does not make a tangent crossing a regular geometric step; the controller still uses AD there.

\section{A decentered arc with global fifth-order convergence}\label{sec:supports}
The fixed circle solves for a correction externally. A second architecture keeps the power endpoint $E=R(s^p)$ and changes the interception support inside the rectangle. Prepare $M=(0,H(1-X^{-1}))$. The slope of $ME$ is $m=H(1-t)/(WX)$. A line through $A$ of slope $k_s=H\kappa/(WX)$ meets the parallel to $ME$ through $P=R(s)$ at $T_A$. The horizontal through $T_A$ gives $P^+$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common}
 s^+=s\frac{k_s}{k_s-m}=s\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1+t}.
\end{equation}
The decentered arc will supply $\kappa=1+g(t)$. The common triangle stage uses horizontals through $B_j=(Ws^j,H(1-s^j))$ on $OB$ and $L_j=L(s^j)$. Parallels to $L_1B$ carry $B_j$ to $L_{j+1}$; the $p$th horizontal gives $E$. This is the triangle protocol of Section~\ref{sec:rails}, not the scaled rail telescope. The classical AK, AD and projective supports are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:supports}; MA remains in Appendix~\ref{app:ma}.

\subsection{Realizing the positive radical}

\subsection{Global monotone convergence of the decentered map}
Unlike the inverse-circle correction, this map is defined for every positive residual and requires no choice between two inverse-model branches. It uses a mobile radius at every step. Unlike rational diagonal Pad\'e, it realizes the correction with a single radical rather than two rational factors. These are practical differences in geometric realization, even though all three maps have fifth local order.

Centered AD and decentered AD use the same mobile support operations: one arc and one join. Their fixed preparations and coordinate scales differ. This is the precise sense in which order five is obtained at the mobile support cost of order three.


\begin{theorem}\label{thm:arc}
For real $p>2$, $X>0$ and every $s_0>0$, \eqref{eq:arc} converges to $a$ monotonically without crossing it. Its logarithmic error satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcorder}
 e^+=\frac{(p-2)(p^2-1)(3p+1)}{1440}e^5+O(e^7).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The radicand is positive and $q(1/t)=q(t)/t$, so the correction is reciprocal. Write
\[
 \mathcal E(t)=\frac{\log t}{p}+\log(1+g(t))-\log(t+g(t)).
\]
Define the auxiliary polynomials
\begin{align*}
 A_*(t)&=(p-1)(t^2+1)+(10p+2)t,\\
 B_*(t)&=(t+1)(6pt-(t-1)^2),\\
 K_*(t)&=(p^2-5p-2)(t-1)^2+12p(3p+1)t.
\end{align*}
Differentiation and factorization give
\begin{align}
 \frac{d\mathcal E}{d\log t}
 &=\frac{(p-2)(p-1)^2(qA_*-B_*)}{12p^2g(1+g)(t+g)},\label{eq:arcder}\\
 q^2A_*^2-B_*^2&=\frac{p+1}{12p}(t-1)^4K_* .\label{eq:arcfactor}
\end{align}
Here $A_*>0$. If $B_*\le0$, then $qA_*-B_*>0$. Otherwise $(t-1)^2<6pt$. When $p^2-5p-2\ge0$, $K_*>0$ directly; otherwise
\[
 K_*=6p^2(p+1)t+(p^2-5p-2)((t-1)^2-6pt)>0.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:arcfactor} now gives $qA_*>B_*$ for $t\ne1$. Therefore $\mathcal E$ is strictly increasing with $\mathcal E(1)=0$: the new logarithmic error has the old sign. Meanwhile $\Phi(t)-1=(1-t)/(t+g)$ moves the state toward the root. The orbit is monotone and bounded between $s_0$ and $a$. Its positive limit is a fixed point by continuity, and $\Phi(t)=1$ implies $t=1$.

Near one, rationalize $qA_*-B_*$ using~\eqref{eq:arcfactor}. Since $q=1$, $g=p-1$, $A_*=B_*=12p$ and $K_*=12p(3p+1)$ at $t=1$, this gives
\[
 \lim_{t\to1}\frac{d\mathcal E/d\log t}{(t-1)^4}
 =\frac{(p-2)(p^2-1)(3p+1)}{288p^5}.
\]
Substituting $t=e^{pe}$ and integrating yields~\eqref{eq:arcorder}; reciprocity makes the analytic logarithmic map odd.
\end{proof}
At $p=3$, the leading coefficient is $+1/18$, whereas the fixed-circle inverse has $-1/18$. The former stays on the starting side; the latter alternates near the root. At $p=2$, both scalar radical formulas reduce to the exact square-root correction, but the displayed decentered preparation must be replaced by a mean-proportional construction.

\subsection{AD entry and geometric exceptions}
Cache $E=R(s^p)$ and $R(t)$. Stop at $t=1$; otherwise apply the chosen step and refresh its power and residual.

\begin{proposition}[Global branch-driven protocol]\label{thm:entry}
For every integer $p\ge3$, $X>0$ and $s_0>0$, this exact geometric protocol converges to $a=X^{-1/p}$. With the preparation specified below, it eventually uses the circle step unless already at the root, and has local order five.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Outside the band, AD converges globally and therefore reaches the open band in finitely many steps. Inside, both maps strictly reduce $|\log t|$ and preserve the band. On the compact log-residual interval determined by entry, both scalar post-error functions are continuous. If the errors tended to $\ell>0$, the larger post-error would be uniformly below the current error on $\ell\le|\log t|\le|\log t_{\rm entry}|$. Every late step would decrease it by a fixed positive amount, a contradiction. Thus chart-driven switching also converges.

The finite chart is regular near the root. For the compact preparation ($p\ne4$), $\rho=1/2$ and
\[
 b(1)=\frac{48p(p+4)(p+1)}{(p-1)\Lambda_p}>0;
\]
for $p=4$, use the separate chart in Appendix~\ref{app:prep}, where direct substitution also gives $\rho\ne1$ and $b(1)\ne0$. At the root $\Delta_p(1)=36p^2>0$. Continuity therefore preserves the transverse crossing, distinct $B,G$ and finite top intersection nearby; the eventual update is the order-five circle map.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}
Two geometric mechanisms realize distinct fifth-order corrections. \subsection{A behavioral baseline and a prospective quadratic cell}
\textbf{Existing order-three baseline.} The V30 behavioral prototype implements centered AD with state $q=p(u-1)$ after a digitally prepared scaling $s=cu$, $Y\approx Xc^p\in[1,2]$. Its residual sensitivity is $\partial\log(Yu^p)/\partial q=1/u$, rather than $p/u$ for an uncentered state. Centered binary products feed the exact update
\[
 q^+=q+\frac{2(1+q/p)(1-t)}{1+t+(t-1)/p},\qquad
 \widehat r=\frac1{c(1+q/p)}.
\]
There are 25 power products at $p=10^6$, and at most 37 over $3\le p\le10^6$. Netlists and calibration are in the companion \cite{analogv30}.
\fig{analog_circuit}{.56}{The retained order-three AD behavioral topology. Memories, loading and switches are explicit; diamonds are arithmetic sources. Neither fifth-order solver has an electrical implementation in this study.}

\textbf{Prospective circle cell.} A candidate feedback cell must enforce $N(v)-tD(v)=0$ on the branch through $v=1$ and form $s^+=sv$. With $z=p(v-1)$, division by 12 gives the exact specification
\[
 0=(1-t)-\frac{(1-2/p)+(1+2/p)t}{2}\,z
 +\frac{(1-3/p+2/p^2)-t(1+3/p+2/p^2)}{12}\,z^2.
\]
Its derivative in $z$ is $-1$ at $(t,z)=(1,0)$, independently of the degree. It vanishes at a branch endpoint, so branch margin and fallback must be designed before this becomes a usable circuit.

\textbf{Evidence retained from V30.} Arithmetic tests cover 8,243 pairs. The calibrated behavioral SPICE campaign gives worst decoded-root error $0.279$ ppm at $23.985$ ms across 64 cases; a $4.785$ ms policy meets one ppm in 18 new cases. At $p=10^6,X=500000$, digital preparation already places $1/c$ within $2.477\times10^{-7}$ relative error. Reported readout times exclude preparation, programming and conversion. These are behavioral simulations, not silicon measurements.

A comparison of the proposed quadratic cell with AD must hold power, memory, loading and noise fixed and measure wrong-branch events, settling and decoded error. Neither faster electronics nor lower energy follows from the geometric join count. This appendix motivates that experiment; it supplies no electrical validation of the fifth-order methods.
