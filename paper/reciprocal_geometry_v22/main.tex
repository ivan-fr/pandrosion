\textbf{Abstract.}
An iteration formula does not specify how to construct its next value. We study explicit line-and-circle realizations for positive roots. A rationally prepared fixed circle acts as a quadratic solver for an inverse Pad\'e correction: the cubic example uses eight mobile joins, and the general linear-power protocol uses $2p+2$. We prove strict logarithmic-error contraction on its entire real inverse branch for every integer $p\ge3$; invariant-band convergence follows, with local order five. Centered AD supplies entry from arbitrary positive starts and handles excluded finite charts. A binary-power realization counts this combined protocol without claiming a global eight-line bound. Five Pandrosion constructions, including linear MA and the globally monotone decentered fifth-order arc, locate the result among explicit geometric alternatives. Finally, we explain how the geometry specifies arithmetic cells, branch selection and state storage for analog design, distinguishing a proposed centered quadratic cell from the existing order-three AD behavioral prototype. Incidence certificates, Lean audits, numerical work tables and executable geometry accompany the paper.

\section{Why construct an iteration?}
A numerical algorithm names operations. A physical calculator must realize them: a ratio may be an alignment, a product a similar-triangle construction, and a square root a circle crossing. Two formulas with the same local order can therefore have different realization costs. This paper asks which geometric arrangements make a root iteration economical and how that arrangement can guide an analog implementation.

The intended setting is constructive computation: exact incidence geometry supplies a design and a correctness argument; a count of mobile operations describes the protocol. It also exposes which quantities must be stored, copied or solved for in a circuit. Drawing cost and electrical delay are different quantities, but the decomposition into reusable operations connects them.

Our principal example is a fixed circle that solves for a correction rather than merely transferring a length. Three joins after the power stage complete a fifth-order update. The circle converges throughout its real branch; a globally defined AD fallback handles entry and geometric exceptions. The decentered arc gives another choice: global monotone convergence with a moving radius. The contribution is these explicit realizations and their analysis, rather than the classical Newton, Halley or Pad\'e families. Section~\ref{sec:scope} collects the proof and implementation boundaries; the main text develops the constructions themselves.

\section{Coordinates as reusable computing elements}\label{sec:rails}
Let $X>0$, let $p\ge3$ be an integer, and write
\[
 a=X^{-1/p},\qquad t=Xs^p,\qquad s^+=s\Phi(t),\qquad s>0.
\]
The state approaches the reciprocal root $a$; inversion supplies the desired $X^{1/p}$. We use $e=\log(s/a)$ for local error. Points are capital letters, scalar iteration maps are $\map{label}$, and polynomial coefficients use Greek letters. Thus a point and a correction function never share the same symbol.

In a rectangle with vertices $C=(0,0)$, $B=(W,0)$, $O=(0,H)$ and $A=(W,H)$, define two extended rails and a family of top centers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rails}
 R(q)=(W,H(1-q)),\quad L(q)=(0,H(1-q)),\quad
 \hub{f}=\left(\frac{Wf}{f-1},H\right).
\end{equation}
A line through the prepared center performs multiplication in one direction and division in the other:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:railops}
 \hub{f}R(q)\cap OC=L(fq),\qquad
 \hub{f}L(b)\cap AB=R(b/f).
\end{equation}
For example, substituting the first two points into a line equation and setting $x=0$ gives ordinate $H(1-fq)$. This is the whole arithmetic content of the rail device.

\subsection{The power stage}
Start at $P=R(s)$. The join $CP$ meets the top line at $V=\hub{1/s}$. A leftward projection through $\hub{1/2}$ followed by a return through $V$ changes a rail value $q$ to $sq/2$. After $p-1$ pairs, the endpoint is $Q=R(s^p/2^{p-1})$. Including the initial join, the stage uses $2p-1$ lines. Its normalization factor is part of the design, not discarded arithmetic.

More generally, choosing the leftward factors $f_1,\ldots,f_{p-1}$ gives an endpoint with value $s^p\prod_i f_i$. This allows the power stage to feed a device with its preferred residual scale. We use exactly this freedom for the fixed-circle calculator.


\textbf{Power protocols, one state encoding.} The later rectangle drawings use a different power stage: similar triangles and parallels produce $E=R(s^p)$ directly. They do not change the meaning of $R(s)$. The rail telescope instead produces a scaled power; its scale is absorbed into the prepared readout. Their costs must be kept separate.

\subsection{A precise notion of work}
We count a join as $J$, a parallel-construction macro as $P_{\parallel}$, and a mobile compass arc as $C_{\mathrm{arc}}$. Intersections of already drawn objects, fixed preparation and final inversion are excluded. When expanded into ruler-and-compass traces, our parallel macro costs $2J+3C_{\mathrm{arc}}$. These conventions are specified before any cost comparison.

This question belongs to the constructive tradition of Lemoine's geom\'etrographie and d'Ocagne's nomography \cite{lemoine1902,docagne1925}. The Poncelet--Steiner theorem supplies a broad existence result for straightedge constructions with a given circle and its center \cite{steiner1833}. Our question is more specific: an explicit repeated protocol, its branch selection, and its mobile cost. The name Pandrosion refers to the addressee of the opening of Book III of Pappus' \emph{Collection} \cite{pappusLittle2023}. Our rectangle follows a modern reconstruction \cite{launayPandrosion}; the accelerated supports are modern constructions, not attributed to the ancient text.

\section{A prepared circle as a quadratic solver}\label{sec:circle}
Suppose the desired correction factor is $v\approx1$. Instead of evaluating a rational approximation to $t^{-1/p}$, approximate the inverse relation $t=v^{-p}$ by a quadratic rational model and solve it exactly. Put
\begin{align}
 \alpha_p&=(p+1)(p+2),&\beta_p&=p^2-4,&\gamma_p&=(p-1)(p-2),\nonumber\\
 N(v)&=\gamma_pv^2-2\beta_pv+\alpha_p,&
 D(v)&=\alpha_pv^2-2\beta_pv+\gamma_p.\label{eq:model}
\end{align}
The model is $\mathcal R(v)=N(v)/D(v)$, the $[2/2]$ Pad\'e approximant of $v^{-p}$ at one. Its inverse branch $v(1)=1$ defines $\map{circle}(s)=sv(Xs^p)$. A circle can supply the quadratic root in a single crossing.

\subsection{The eight-line cubic example}
Use $W=2$, $H=4$, $p=3$ and $X=2$. Prepare a circle through $B$ with center $O_\Gamma$, a projector $Z$, and constants
\[
 O_\Gamma=(-152/113,126/113),\quad Z=(478/113,396/113),
 \qquad \rho=\tfrac12,\quad k=\tfrac5{113}.
\]
The first five joins form the rail power stage with left factors $\rho$ and $\sigma=20/113$, retaining $L_1=L(\rho s)$ and ending at $T=R(kXs^3)$. The last three joins are the main device:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw $ZT$ and select its prescribed intersection $G$ with the fixed circle.
\item Draw $BG$ to the top line, obtaining $U=\hub{\rho/v}$.
\item Draw $UL_1$ back to the right rail. Its value is $(\rho s)/(\rho/v)=sv$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the inverse correction has order five}
The local order follows from one polynomial identity, which is also a useful certificate for the approximation. At $v=1$, $N=D=12$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
 pND+v(N'D-ND')=p\alpha_p\gamma_p(v-1)^4.
\end{equation}
Moreover $\gamma_pN=(\gamma_pv-\beta_p)^2+3(p^2-4)$ and $\alpha_pD=(\alpha_pv-\beta_p)^2+3(p^2-4)$, so the logarithms used below are well defined for $p>2$.

\begin{theorem}[Local inverse-circle order]
For real $p>2$, the inverse branch through $v=1$ exists near $t=1$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circleorder}
 e^+=-\frac{(p^2-1)(p^2-4)}{720}e^5+O(e^7).
\end{equation}
It therefore alternates sides locally and has exact order five.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $v=1+u$, $E(u)=\log\mathcal R(1+u)/p$ and $K(u)=E(u)+\log(1+u)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:cert} gives
\[
 K'(u)=\frac{\alpha_p\gamma_pu^4}{(1+u)N(1+u)D(1+u)}.
\]
Thus $K(u)/u^5\to\alpha_p\gamma_p/720$, while $E(0)=0$ and $E'(0)=-1$. The inverse function theorem identifies $E(u)$ with the input logarithmic error. Dividing the limits gives the coefficient in~\eqref{eq:circleorder}. Analyticity and $\mathcal R(1/v)=1/\mathcal R(v)$ make the logarithmic update odd, giving the stated remainder. The coefficient is nonzero for $p>2$.
\end{proof}
The same identity, integrated as the derivative of $\log(v^p\mathcal R(v))$, proves the fifth-order Pad\'e contact. Model-based iteration and quadratic approximants have classical precedents \cite{traub1961,traub1964,shafer1974}; the construction problem is to realize this inverse with a short, prepared geometric device.

\subsection{Which circle intersection?}
The discriminant after removing a factor four is
\[
 \Delta_p(t)=(42p^2-24)t-3(p^2-4)(1+t^2).
\]
The transverse real interval is $\tau_-<t<\tau_+$, where
\[
 \tau_\pm=\frac{7p^2-4\pm4p\sqrt{3(p^2-1)}}{p^2-4},\qquad \tau_-\tau_+=1.
\]
Select the descending branch $\mathcal R'(v)<0$, equivalently
\[
 v_-<v<v_+,\qquad v_\pm=\frac{p^2+2\pm\sqrt{12(p^2-1)}}{p^2-4}.
\]
Its reference point is $v=1$. At $p=3,t=0.05$, both inverse roots are positive, approximately 3.1183 and 6.3817; only the first is on this branch. Positivity alone cannot choose $G$. At a transverse endpoint the two roots merge. Theorem~\ref{thm:branch} below proves strict error contraction on this entire interval. Tangencies remain excluded from the finite protocol, and the real interval is not the whole positive axis.

\subsection{The general-degree construction}\label{sec:generalcircle}
For each integer $p\ge3$, prepare $O_\Gamma=(h,j)$, $Z=(z_x,z_y)$, $k>0$ and $\rho>0$, using the rational formulas in Appendix~\ref{app:prep}. The circle passes through $B=(2,0)$ and is independent of $X$ and $s$. Put $\sigma=kX2^{p-3}/\rho$. The rail sequence uses
\[
 f_1=\rho,\quad f_i=\tfrac12\ (2\le i\le p-2),\quad f_{p-1}=\sigma.
\]
It saves $L_1=L(\rho s)$ and ends at $T=R(kt)$, because $\prod_i f_i=kX$. The same three final joins then give $R(sv)$.

\begin{theorem}[Counted finite-circle protocol]\label{thm:construction}
For the preparation in Appendix~\ref{app:prep}, assume $X,s>0$, $\tau_-<Xs^p<\tau_+$, and
\[
 s\ne1,\quad \sigma\ne1,\quad v\ne\rho,\quad
 b(v)=(2-h)2v-4j(\rho-v)\ne0.
\]
The selected branch is realized by $2p+2$ mobile joins using the prepared circle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The power stage costs $1+2(p-1)=2p-1$ joins. To certify the remaining incidence, write
\[
 d=(2v,4(\rho-v)),\quad n=d\cdot d,\quad b=(B-O_\Gamma)\cdot d,
 \quad G=B-\frac{2b}{n}d.
\]
The pencil line through $B$ has direction $d$. Substituting $B+\xi d$ into the circle equation gives $\xi(\xi n+2b)=0$: besides $B$, the other intersection is exactly the displayed $G$. Thus $b\ne0$ keeps the two points distinct. The same line meets the top at $\hub{\rho/v}$ when $v\ne\rho$.

It remains to show that the projector $Z$ and the residual point $T$ select this $G$. Three points are collinear exactly when the determinant of their displacement vectors vanishes. Multiplying that determinant by $n$ gives
\begin{align*}
 I={}&(2-z_x)(-2bd_y-z_yn)\\
 &-[4(1-kt)-z_y][(2-z_x)n-2bd_x].
\end{align*}
The prepared parameters satisfy $I=\lambda_p[N(v)-tD(v)]$, with $\lambda_p\ne0$. This identity converts the geometric incidence into exactly the inverse model, rather than an approximation to it. Since the selected root has $N(v)-tD(v)=0$, it places $G$ on $ZT$. Projecting $G$ through $B$, then $L_1$ through the resulting center, gives $R(sv)$ by~\eqref{eq:railops}. These are three joins. The branch arc is parameterized by $v_-<v<v_+$ and can be marked during preparation using its endpoints and $G(1)$. The listed hypotheses exclude an infinite center, a coincident $B,G$, and tangency; the other rail intersections are transverse.
\end{proof}
The exclusions concern this finite chart. In particular, $s=1$ sends the initial center $V$ to infinity even though the scalar correction can remain regular. A positive rescaling $\widetilde X=c^pX$, $\widetilde s=s/c$ preserves $t$ and conjugates every correction. A dyadic choice can keep the limiting state away from one, controlling the distant initial center near convergence. The circle-specific exclusions still require their own branch and chart checks.

The result is a concrete upper bound on mobile work, not merely an existence theorem for straightedge construction. Its advantage over the rational fifth-order baseline will be quantified after the other devices have been introduced.

\subsection{Strict error contraction on the whole real branch}\label{sec:branch}
The finite real interval is a convergence region, not merely a domain on which a square root exists. The result below closes the whole-branch conjecture stated in V20. ``Contraction'' here means strict reduction of the error relative to the root; it does not assert a common Lipschitz constant between arbitrary states.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-branch contraction]\label{thm:branch}
Let $p\ge3$ be an integer and $\tau_-<t<\tau_+$, $t\ne1$. For the descending inverse branch $v=v(t)$,
\[
 \left|\frac{\log t}{p}+\log v\right|<\frac{|\log t|}{p}.
\]
Consequently the scalar circle iteration stays inside this residual band and converges to $X^{-1/p}$ from every start in it. Its local order is five.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Reduce the error comparison to a sign.} Parameterize the branch by $v$, so $t=N(v)/D(v)$. Put
\[
 h(v)=p\log v+2\log\frac{N(v)}{D(v)},\qquad
 S(v)=2\alpha_p\gamma_p(v-1)^4-N(v)D(v).
\]
The difference of squared errors is $\log v\,h(v)/p$. It suffices to prove $h(v)<0$ for $1<v<v_+$; reciprocity gives $h(1/v)=-h(v)$ and handles the other half. Equivalently, for integer $p$ the desired sign is $(v-1)[D(v)^2-v^pN(v)^2]>0$.

\textbf{Control the shape of $h$.} The certificate~\eqref{eq:cert} gives
\[
 h'(v)=\frac{pS(v)}{vN(v)D(v)}.
\]
Write $v=1+u$. The useful simplification is
\[
 \frac{S(1+u)}{u^2}=\alpha_p\gamma_pu^2+12(p^2-4)u+12(p^2-16)-\frac{288}{u}-\frac{144}{u^2}.
\]
For $u>0$, its derivative is strictly positive. Since $S(1)=-144$, $h$ first decreases and can then increase only once. Thus $h(1)=0$ and the single endpoint inequality $h(v_+)<0$ imply $h(v)<0$ throughout $(1,v_+)$. The next page proves that endpoint inequality for every integer degree.

\textbf{Pass from one step to convergence.} Let $z=\log t$ and $F(z)=\log[tv(t)^p]$. The inverse branch is continuous in the open band, $F(0)=0$, and $|F(z)|<|z|$ for $z\ne0$. Since $\tau_-\tau_+=1$, an orbit starting at $z_0$ remains in the compact interval $[-|z_0|,|z_0|]$ strictly inside the band. Its absolute errors decrease to a limit $\ell$. If $\ell>0$, a convergent subsequence has limit $z_*$ with $|z_*|=\ell$; continuity would give both $|F(z_*)|=\ell$ and $|F(z_*)|<\ell$. Hence $\ell=0$. The local order follows from~\eqref{eq:circleorder}.
\end{proof}
The first, strict-error assertion is \texttt{whole\_branch\_contraction} in \texttt{RectangleFixedCircleBranch}; its 33-declaration audit checks the sign and endpoint argument. The invariant-band convergence and inverse-branch assembly above are written corollaries. This distinction avoids calling the entire analytic/geometric program a single formal theorem.

\subsection{An endpoint certificate valid at every degree}\label{sec:endpoint}
Let $r=\sqrt{12(p^2-1)}$, $u_+=(6+r)/(p^2-4)$ and $v_+=1+u_+$. At this branch endpoint,
\[
 (p^2-4)u_+^2=12(u_++1),\qquad \frac{N(v_+)}{D(v_+)}=\tau_-.
\]
Therefore $h(v_+)<0$ is exactly $v_+^p<\tau_+^2$. The quadratic relation reduces the polynomials to linear expressions:
\begin{align*}
 N(1+u_+)&=\frac{6[2(2p+1)-(p^2-2p-2)u_+]}{p+2},\\
 D(1+u_+)&=\frac{6[2(2p-1)+(p^2+2p-2)u_+]}{p-2}.
\end{align*}
For $3\le p\le9$, rational brackets for $r$ give exact rational upper bounds on $v_+^pN(v_+)^2/D(v_+)^2$ below one:

\section{The five Pandrosion constructions}\label{sec:supports}
The fixed circle solves for a correction externally. A second architecture keeps the power endpoint $E=R(s^p)$ and changes the interception support inside the rectangle. Prepare $M=(0,H(1-X^{-1}))$. The slope of $ME$ is $m=H(1-t)/(WX)$. A line through $A$ of slope $k_s=H\kappa/(WX)$ meets the parallel to $ME$ through $P=R(s)$ at $T_A$. The horizontal through $T_A$ gives $P^+$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common}
 s^+=s\frac{k_s}{k_s-m}=s\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1+t}.
\end{equation}
This one identity explains the four accelerated supports; each is a different device for supplying $\kappa$. Here we deliberately use the triangle protocol from Section~\ref{sec:rails}, rather than the rail telescope: horizontals meet the diagonal $OB$ at $B_j=(Ws^j,H(1-s^j))$ and the left rail at $L_j=L(s^j)$. Parallels to $L_1B$ carry $B_j$ to $L_{j+1}$, by similar triangles. The $p$th horizontal gives $E=R(s^p)$.

\textbf{Reference construction: linear MA.}
MA provides a baseline, not an additional accelerated method. Write its lower-right endpoint as $A_{\rm lin}=B$. The parallel to $ME$ through $P$ meets $MB$; a horizontal returns the next state. For $s\ne1$,
\[
 \map{MA}(s)=1-\frac{X-1}{X S_p(s)},\qquad S_p(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}s^j.
\]
For $X>1$ this scalar map is increasing and $\map{MA}(s)-s=(1-Xs^p)/(XS_p(s))$. It therefore converges monotonically to $a$ from every positive start, with the removable scalar extension at $s=1$. Its derivative at $a$ is $1-pa^{p-1}/S_p(a)\in(0,1)$: convergence is linear.

The green parallel meets the blue support $MA_{\rm lin}$; the horizontal gives $P^+$. The historical endpoint $A_{\rm lin}$ is the lower-right vertex $B$ in this paper. At $s=1$ the geometric intersection is coincident despite the removable scalar extension.}

\subsection{AK and centered AD: orders two and three}

\textbf{AK: a fixed line.} Prepare $K=(W(1-X/p),0)$. The support $AK$ has $\kappa=p$, so $\map{AK}(s)=ps/(p-1+t)$, Newton under inversion. It has order two and converges from every positive start.

\textbf{AD: a centered length transfer.} Prepare
\[
 O_{\mathrm{AD}}=\left(W-\frac{2W}{p-1},\ H-\frac{H(p+1)}{X(p-1)}\right).
\]
Transfer $AE=Hs^p$ downward from this center to $D_{\mathrm{AD}}$ and join $AD_{\mathrm{AD}}$. Then $\kappa=[p+1+(p-1)t]/2$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:halley}
 \map{AD}(s)=s\frac{p+1+(p-1)t}{p-1+(p+1)t}.
\end{equation}
This is Halley, with cubic local order and global monotone positive convergence. Geometric interpretations of Halley precede this construction \cite{gander1985,scavo1995}; the support here is specified to preserve the common rail encoding and readout.

\subsection{Projective AD: order four}
\textbf{Two joins supply the support.} Prepare
\[
 \ell:\ y=H\left(1+\frac{5p-1}{(p+1)X}\right),\quad
 Z_{\mathrm{proj}}=\left(W\left(1+\frac{5p-1}{2p(2p-1)}\right),H\left(1+\frac{p+1}{(2p-1)X}\right)\right).
\]
Set $D_{\mathrm{proj}}=Z_{\mathrm{proj}}E\cap\ell$ and join $AD_{\mathrm{proj}}$. Substitution in~\eqref{eq:common} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proj}
 \map{proj}(s)=s\frac{2p((2p-1)t+p+1)}{(p+1)t^2+2(2p-1)(p+1)t+(2p-1)(p-1)}.
\end{equation}
The direct-root correction is Pad\'e $[2/1]$; the reciprocal-state factor displayed here is $[1/2]$. Denote its positive denominator by $Q_{\mathrm{proj}}(t)$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \map{proj}'(s)=-\frac{2p(p^2-1)(2p-1)(t-1)^3}{Q_{\mathrm{proj}}(t)^2}.
\]
The map has its global maximum $a$ at $s=a$. Also $\map{proj}(s)/s-1=(1-t)((p+1)t+5p-1)/Q_{\mathrm{proj}}(t)$. Thus a first step lands at or below $a$, and subsequent steps increase to $a$. Integrating the derivative at $a$ gives exact order four. The archival companion gives further dynamics and the original triangle-chain realization \cite{geometryv20}.

\subsection{A decentered arc with a global fifth-order map}
For $p>2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arc}
 q(t)=\sqrt{\frac{(p-2)(t^2+1)+(10p+4)t}{12p}},\quad g(t)=(p-1)q(t),\quad
 \map{arc}(s)=s\frac{1+g(t)}{t+g(t)}.
\end{equation}
For integer $p\ge3$, put $\eta=(p-1)\sqrt{(p-2)/(12p)}$, $\zeta=(5p+2)/(p-2)$, $d_0=W/\eta$ and $\delta=(H/X)\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}$. Prepare
\[
 G_0=(W,H+H\zeta/X),\quad
 O_{\mathrm{arc}}=(W-d_0+\delta,H-H/(X\eta)),\quad x=W-d_0.
\]
Draw the arc centered at $O_{\mathrm{arc}}$ with radius $EG_0=(H/X)(t+\zeta)$ and take the lower intersection $D_{\mathrm{arc}}$ with the prepared vertical. Its vertical distance below the center is
\[
 \sqrt{EG_0^2-\delta^2}=\frac HX\sqrt{t^2+2\zeta t+1}.
\]
The support $AD_{\mathrm{arc}}$ consequently gives $\kappa=1+g(t)$ in~\eqref{eq:common}. All preparation parameters use rational operations and square roots of positive quantities. The radicand stays positive for $t>0$.

These are practical differences in geometric realization, even though all three maps have fifth local order.

Centered AD and decentered AD use the same mobile support operations: one arc and one join. Their fixed preparations and coordinate scales differ. This is the precise sense in which order five is obtained at the mobile support cost of order three.

\subsection{Global monotone convergence of the decentered map}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:arc}
For real $p>2$, $X>0$ and every $s_0>0$, \eqref{eq:arc} converges to $a$ monotonically without crossing it. Its logarithmic error satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcorder}
 e^+=\frac{(p-2)(p^2-1)(3p+1)}{1440}e^5+O(e^7).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The radicand is positive and $q(1/t)=q(t)/t$, so the correction is reciprocal. Write
\[
 \mathcal E(t)=\frac{\log t}{p}+\log(1+g(t))-\log(t+g(t)).
\]
Define the auxiliary polynomials
\begin{align*}
 A_*(t)&=(p-1)(t^2+1)+(10p+2)t,\\
 B_*(t)&=(t+1)(6pt-(t-1)^2),\\
 K_*(t)&=(p^2-5p-2)(t-1)^2+12p(3p+1)t.
\end{align*}
Differentiation and factorization give
\begin{align}
 \frac{d\mathcal E}{d\log t}
 &=\frac{(p-2)(p-1)^2(qA_*-B_*)}{12p^2g(1+g)(t+g)},\label{eq:arcder}\\
 q^2A_*^2-B_*^2&=\frac{p+1}{12p}(t-1)^4K_* .\label{eq:arcfactor}
\end{align}
Here $A_*>0$. If $B_*\le0$, then $qA_*-B_*>0$. Otherwise $(t-1)^2<6pt$. When $p^2-5p-2\ge0$, $K_*>0$ directly; otherwise
\[
 K_*=6p^2(p+1)t+(p^2-5p-2)((t-1)^2-6pt)>0.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:arcfactor} now gives $qA_*>B_*$ for $t\ne1$. Therefore $\mathcal E$ is strictly increasing with $\mathcal E(1)=0$: the new logarithmic error has the old sign. Meanwhile $\Phi(t)-1=(1-t)/(t+g)$ moves the state toward the root. The orbit is monotone and bounded between $s_0$ and $a$. Its positive limit is a fixed point by continuity, and $\Phi(t)=1$ implies $t=1$.

Near one, rationalize $qA_*-B_*$ using~\eqref{eq:arcfactor}. Since $q=1$, $g=p-1$, $A_*=B_*=12p$ and $K_*=12p(3p+1)$ at $t=1$, this gives
\[
 \lim_{t\to1}\frac{d\mathcal E/d\log t}{(t-1)^4}
 =\frac{(p-2)(p^2-1)(3p+1)}{288p^5}.
\]
Substituting $t=e^{pe}$ and integrating yields~\eqref{eq:arcorder}; reciprocity makes the analytic logarithmic map odd.
\end{proof}
At $p=3$, the leading coefficient is $+1/18$, whereas the fixed-circle inverse has $-1/18$. The former stays on the starting side; the latter alternates near the root. At $p=2$, both scalar radical formulas reduce to the exact square-root correction, but the displayed decentered preparation must be replaced by a mean-proportional construction.

\section{What the shorter construction saves}\label{sec:cost}
A useful comparison holds the state, requested accuracy and counting convention fixed. The rational competitors can be built on the same rail telescope. A prepared projector
\[
 Z(\mu,\nu)=\left(-\frac{W\mu}{1-\mu},H\left(1-\frac{\nu}{1-\mu}\right)\right)
\]
sends $Q=R(q_p)$ to $\hub{f(q_p)}$, with $f(q)=\mu+\nu/q$. Two such projections and two rail joins multiply a stored value by $f/g$: a four-join module.

For Halley choose $f=(p-1)/(4p)+(p+1)/(4pt)$ and $g=(p+1)/(4p)+(p-1)/(4pt)$. Since $t=X2^{p-1}q_p$, each is of the required projector form. The cost is $2p-1+4=2p+3$ joins.

\[
 \Phi_{\mathrm{rat}}(t)=\frac{a_5t^2+b_5t+c_5}{c_5t^2+b_5t+a_5}

\section{From a convergence band to all positive starts}\label{sec:safeguard}
The whole-branch theorem removes the need to test the improvement of a correctly selected, regular circle step. It does not create real intersections outside the band or remove affine chart singularities. We therefore distinguish two global protocols: a branch-driven one using the new theorem, and the residual-controlled V21 protocol whose formal switching proof is independent of it.

\subsection{AD entry and geometric exceptions}
Cache $E=R(s^p)$ and $R(t)$, and stop if $t=1$. Attempt the descending circle readout. Use it whenever the crossing is transverse and its readout finite; otherwise use centered AD. Refresh the power and residual of the output. Thus AD supplies entry to the band and any exceptional readouts, rather than checking every circle step's improvement.

\begin{proposition}[Global branch-driven protocol]\label{thm:entry}
For every integer $p\ge3$, $X>0$ and $s_0>0$, this exact geometric protocol converges to $a=X^{-1/p}$. With the preparation specified below, it eventually uses the circle step unless already at the root, and has local order five.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Outside the real band all steps are AD. Its global convergence brings the residual into the open band after finitely many steps. Inside, the circle strictly reduces $|\log t|$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:branch}; AD also strictly reduces it away from the root. Both preserve the band.

Chart exceptions can switch between these maps. They cannot prevent convergence: on the compact symmetric log-residual interval determined by the first entry, both scalar post-error functions are continuous. If the decreasing errors had limit $\ell>0$, their maximum would be uniformly smaller than the current error on the compact set $\ell\le|\log t|\le|\log t_{\rm entry}|$. Every late step would decrease the error by a fixed positive amount, a contradiction. Hence the error tends to zero.

The finite chart is regular near the root. For the compact preparation ($p\ne4$), $\rho=1/2$ and
\[
 b(1)=\frac{48p(p+4)(p+1)}{(p-1)\Lambda_p}>0;
\]
for $p=4$, use the separate chart in Appendix~\ref{app:prep}, where direct substitution also gives $\rho\ne1$ and $b(1)\ne0$. Geometrically, $v\ne\rho$ keeps $BG$ from being parallel to the top rail, while $b\ne0$ keeps $G$ distinct from $B$. At the root $\Delta_p(1)=36p^2>0$, so continuity preserves all three conditions nearby. The eventual update is therefore the order-five circle map.
\end{proof}
This proposition's switching/compactness assembly is a written proof. Lean checks the strict branch inequality and the chart-at-root certificates separately. The fully formal global controller remains the next, more conservative protocol.

\subsection{The residual gate remains an independent option}
For a positive circle candidate $c$, construct $u=Xc^p$ and accept precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate}
 (t\le1\ \wedge\ t\le u^2\ \wedge\ tu^2\le1)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 (1\le t\ \wedge\ u^2\le t\ \wedge\ 1\le tu^2).
\end{equation}
Otherwise use AD. Three rail comparisons implement this condition, equivalent to $|\log u|\le|\log t|/2$. The errors decrease; if their limit were positive, eventually no halving step would be possible, leaving only AD and forcing the limit to zero. This is the V21 global switching theorem, including the incidence-based gate, formalized in \texttt{RectangleCircleSafeguard}. The strict branch theorem alone does not assert this stronger half-error inequality. The gate is retained as an independently justified controller, not as a necessary test inside the proved branch.

\subsection{Geometric arithmetic and complete trace counts}
The controller uses a finite, five-join product. Given $R(a),R(b)$ with $b>0$, test $R(b)=B$; if equal, reuse $R(a)$. Otherwise $CR(b)$ gives $V=\hub{1/b}$. Project $R(a)$ through $\hub{1/2}$ to $L(a/2)$; its join to $V$ gives $R(ab/2)$. Project through $\hub{1/2}$ and then $\hub{1/4}$ to obtain $L(ab/4)$ and $R(ab)$. The exact unit shortcut handles the otherwise infinite center.

A fixed positive scale $d$ uses two joins, through $\hub{d/[2(1+d)]}$ and $\hub{1/[2(1+d)]}$. Binary powering with $m=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor+\operatorname{popcount}(p)-1$ products therefore constructs $R(s^p)$ and $R(Xs^p)$ in at most $5m+2$ joins, including when $s=1$. Both residual protocols use this power stage.

\section{Binary geometry and the path to an analog calculator}\label{sec:binary}
The linear telescope is attractive for a few degrees. At large $p$, binary powering gives a smaller arithmetic architecture. Define a top encoding $Z_\times(q)=(W+Hq,H)$ and the fixed unit $U_\times=(W+H,H)$. A parallel to $U_\times B$ copies $R(a)$ to $Z_\times(a)$. The parallel through $Z_\times(a)$ to $U_\times R(b)$ then meets the right rail at $R(ab)$: its slope is $b$ and its horizontal displacement is $-Ha$.

Squaring the accumulator and multiplying by $s$ at each binary one produces $E=R(s^p)$. With $L=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor$ and $h=\operatorname{popcount}(p)$, there are $m=L+h-1$ multiplications. Reusing the top copy of $s$, the power stage costs $mJ+(m+L)P_{\parallel}$. It feeds any of the four rectangle supports unchanged.

\subsection{From an incidence equation to an analog cell}
\textbf{What has been implemented.} The V30 prototype realizes centered AD, of order three. A digitally prepared scale $c$ makes $Y\approx Xc^p\in[1,2]$, with $s=cu$. Storing $u\approx1$ directly would give sensitivity $\partial\log(Yu^p)/\partial u=p/u$. Instead store $q=p(u-1)$: the sensitivity becomes $1/u$. Centered binary products evaluate the residual, followed by
\[
 q^+=q+\frac{2(1+q/p)(1-t)}{1+t+(t-1)/p},\qquad
 \widehat r=\frac1{c(1+q/p)}.
\]
The V30 companion gives the exact centered product recurrences, netlists and calibration protocol \cite{analogv30}. There are 25 power products at $p=10^6$, and at most 37 over the programmed range $3\le p\le10^6$.
\fig{analog_circuit}{.73}{Existing AD behavioral topology from V30. State memory, loading and switches are explicit; diamonds are behavioral arithmetic sources. The drawing identifies where state must settle and be retained. It is the implemented order-three baseline, not an electrical implementation of the circle solver.}

\textbf{What the fixed circle specifies for a new cell.} Its geometric incidence is an exact circuit specification: solve $N(v)-tD(v)=0$ on the branch through $v=1$, then form $s^+=sv$. A circle on paper does not by itself supply an electrical component. A candidate realization would use multipliers, weighted sums and feedback to enforce this quadratic equation, with branch selection and AD fallback.

As with AD, the useful electrical variable at large $p$ is centered: put $z=p(v-1)$. Dividing the incidence equation by 12 gives the exact relation
\begin{align*}
 0={}&(1-t)-\frac{(1-2/p)+(1+2/p)t}{2}\,z\\
 &+\frac{(1-3/p+2/p^2)-t(1+3/p+2/p^2)}{12}\,z^2 .
\end{align*}
At $(t,z)=(1,0)$ the derivative of the left side with respect to $z$ is $-1$, independently of $p$. This removes the trivial large-degree scaling from the local quadratic cell. At a branch endpoint that derivative vanishes: an electrical design must restrict its operating margin or fall back before the tangency. Algebraic centering alone does not control noise or feedback settling.

\subsection{What the prototype teaches about the construction}
The existing evidence answers three distinct design questions. The 8,243-pair arithmetic campaign checks that one programmable binary schedule covers many degrees and input scales. The calibrated behavioral SPICE study meets one ppm decoded-root error in 64 cases, with worst error 0.279 ppm at 23.985 ms: memory, calibration and readout determine a practical error floor even when the iteration is exact. A 4.785 ms policy meets one ppm on 18 new cases, demonstrating a precision--time tradeoff within that same behavioral model, not a measured silicon speed.

Preparation can dominate an apparently impressive large-degree example. At $p=10^6,X=500000$, the digitally prepared $1/c$ is already within about $2.477\times10^{-7}$ relative error of the root. The analog loop refines it. A fair comparison must therefore include preparation, programming and conversion; the recorded circuit readout times exclude those operations.

For the proposed circle cell, the next experiment is consequently specific: retain the same power module and memories, replace the AD correction by the centered quadratic relation, and measure wrong-branch events, settling time and decoded-root error under the same loading and noise assumptions. Fifth-order mathematical convergence can reduce the number of updates; the extra nonlinear solve may cost time and energy per update. The geometry supplies an exact operation graph and testable cell equations. A performance advantage requires that electrical comparison, which has not yet been performed.

\section{Conclusion}
A rationally prepared circle realizes a fifth-order correction in $2p+2$ joins, and its selected inverse branch strictly contracts logarithmic error throughout the real band. AD entry and chart fallback extend the algorithm to arbitrary positive starts, with a separately counted cost. The five Pandrosion supports and rational pencil baselines show how geometry redistributes the arithmetic work.

The same construction language specifies analog cells and their dependencies. Centering the circle's quadratic relation gives a degree-independent derivative at the root; implementing and characterizing that cell remains the next electrical task. The existing centered AD prototype supplies a reproducible baseline. Better-conditioned charts, lower control costs and a measured quadratic-cell error/settling budget are the remaining priorities.
